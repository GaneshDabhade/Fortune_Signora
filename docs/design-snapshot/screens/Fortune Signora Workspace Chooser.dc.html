Workspace Chooser

How Signora decides which workspace a person is in, and the two surfaces where that decision becomes visible. The chooser is deliberately the least-used of them: eight ordered selection rules resolve the great majority of entries without asking, and the chooser exists for the residue where the system genuinely cannot decide. Two surfaces, not one — the shell switcher for a person who already has context, and the standalone chooser for a person who has none.

Eight rules, ordered, first match wins

Nothing about selection is implicit. Given a membership list, a URL and stored state, both the person and the implementation can predict the outcome before it happens. The function is pure: it reads state that is already loaded and makes no network call, so it can be unit-tested exhaustively across the eight branches.

03 · Persistence & two surfaces

What is stored, where, and for how long

Three separate things are often conflated: the pinned default, the last-used workspace, and the workspace the current tab is in. They have different scopes and different lifetimes, and treating them as one is the source of the “it keeps opening the wrong workspace” complaint.

Recency is per device, deliberately. A phone and a laptop may sit in different workspaces without one overwriting the other, which is the normal case for anyone who checks Signora on the way to work.

Two surfaces, one selection engine

Check again Email your IT team
Creating a workspace is not offered here. Your account is managed by Northwind, and a workspace you created yourself would sit outside their administration.
The suppression is the point. Offering “create a workspace” to an SSO identity whose group mapping has not run produces a shadow tenant and splits the company in two — the single most damaging outcome available on this screen.
