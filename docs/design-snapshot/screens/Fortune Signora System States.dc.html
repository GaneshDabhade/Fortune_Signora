System states

These are the screens a person sees on the worst day they will have with the product. They are also the screens most likely to be built last, by whoever is closest, with an apology and a cartoon. Fortune Signora treats them as a designed system: fifteen states, one grammar, and a rule that every one of them answers three questions before it offers a button.

01 · Scope ladder

A failure claims the smallest surface it can

The single most common error-handling defect in enterprise software is a widget failure that takes the whole page. One chart cannot reach its service, and a person loses their filters, their scroll position and their unsaved edit. Scope is therefore chosen deliberately, and escalating is a decision with a reason.

07 · Loading skeletons & AI processing

Three loading tiers, chosen by duration

A skeleton shown for 80ms is a flash of noise; a spinner shown for 8 seconds is an unanswered question. The tier is picked from the measured p75 of the request, not by preference.

{{ t.range }}
{{ t.t }}

The skeleton is the real layout: same row heights, same column positions, same padding. When data lands nothing moves, because a layout that reflows on arrival costs a mis-click.
AI processing

Model work takes seconds, not milliseconds, so it never uses a spinner. The steps are named as they run — the difference between a system that is thinking and a system that is stuck is legible only if it tells you what it is doing.
