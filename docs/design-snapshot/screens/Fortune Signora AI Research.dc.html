Why is EMEA enterprise slipping?

Asked by Marta Reinhardt · today 09:02 ·v3 ·14 sources · 3 excluded ·{{ headerPips }}Confidence 4 of 5
Generated report Editable · your edits are preserved through regeneration OutlineComments 2
Answer

Three of eleven open EMEA enterprise renewals are slipping, and they share one mechanism rather than three separate problems: each raised a pricing objection with an economic buyer1, then moved a close date within seven days2, and in none of them had procurement been engaged before the objection3. Together they hold $2.42M of the $8.34M currently at risk.

The mechanism

In each deal the champion was an operational buyer who could not approve the uplift. When price entered the conversation, the decision moved to procurement — a function with no relationship to us and a mandate to renegotiate4. Two of the three then saw the champion physically move into that function.

What worked elsewhere

Across 412 FY26 renewals, engaging procurement before the second review raised win rate from 41% to 63%5. Where the reframe used regulatory cost rather than discount, cycle time fell 23 days.

Recommendation

Treat this as one play, not three rescues: introduce procurement to the EU emissions-reporting deadline in all three accounts this week, before the next review cycle closes
