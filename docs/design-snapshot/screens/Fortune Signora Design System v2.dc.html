Version 2.0 · Quiet Premium Supersedes v1.0 · Inherits its tokens and rules

A calmer, more confident Fortune Signora

Version 1 was structurally right and visually austere: correct grids, flat planes, hard rules, zero radius. Version 2 keeps the rigour and the density, and rebuilds the surface language — executive blue, warm neutrals, sculpted corners, layered depth, and AI embedded in the ordinary path of work rather than announced beside it.

Colour system

Executive blue carries the brand; warm neutrals carry the page. The warmth matters — v1's cool greys were what made it read as government software. 04

Typography

Archivo stays, but the register changes. v1 set headings at 800 with tight leading, which read as a poster; v2 sets them at 700 with optical tracking that tightens as size grows, and gives body copy a 1.6 line-height for long working sessions. Weight 600 replaces 800 for every interface label — the difference between a system that whispers and one that shouts.

05

Space & radius

The 8-point scale survives intact; what changes is generosity — card padding moves from 16 to 20/24, section rhythm from 48 to 64, and page margins from 32 to 40. Radius is no longer zero. Every container is sculpted on a five-step scale where the value rises with the surface's size and transience: a chip is nearly round, a dialog is softly cornered, a table is a single rounded plane with its rows squared inside it.

06

Elevation

v1 forbade shadow on anchored surfaces, which is why it looked printed. v2 uses a two-part recipe on every raised plane: a hairline border for definition plus a soft, wide, low-opacity shadow for lift. Anchored cards sit at --e1 — visible but weightless. Nothing blurs, nothing goes translucent, no shadow is ever tinted.

07

Navigation

Same capability, a lighter hand. The rail keeps nine destinations and two levels but sits on a slightly lighter ground with no dividing rule, items are 8px-radius pills with a warm hover, and the active item is a filled brand-tint pill rather than a bar on an edge. The top bar loses its 2px rule and its uppercase, gains a workspace switcher and an assistant entry, and carries a single 32px search field that opens the palette.

15

Agent workflows

An agent is a named, scoped, interruptible sequence with a visible plan. The plan is shown before it runs, each step reports what it read and what it produced, anything that writes to a system of record pauses for approval, and the whole run is reversible and appears in the record timeline as agent-authored. Long runs continue in the background with a completion toast — the user is never held hostage to a progress bar.

Motion

One curve — cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) — and three durations. Motion is a hint about origin and continuity, never a performance. Anything that grows uses progressive disclosure: the container animates its height, the content fades in 60ms behind it, and nothing shifts what the user was already reading.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA is preserved in full and the margins are wider than v1's. Softer surfaces did not cost contrast: body ink sits at 14:1, secondary ink at 7:1, brand on white at 7.4:1, and every tint pill pairs with a 600-weight label in a colour that clears 4.5:1 against it. Radius and shadow are decorative layers only — nothing depends on them to be understood.

Migration from v1

v1 is not deprecated; it is the substrate. Its grid, breakpoints, spacing base, token tiers, status vocabulary and four rule sets are inherited verbatim. What changes is the surface language, and almost all of it lands through tokens rather than markup.

The contract still holds

Modules inherit these tokens and components without restating them, and components are not redesigned per team or per quarter. Version 2 exists because the surface language needed a generational change, not because the rules were wrong — the rules are why the change could be made in tokens rather than in a rewrite. The next change of this size should be a v3, argued in the open, with a migration attached.
